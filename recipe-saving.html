Recipe Saving & Copyright

Twelve Geese is a personal cookbook: a digital version of the folder where people keep printed recipes, magazine cut-outs and handwritten cards. Here is how saving recipes from elsewhere works, and the principles behind it.

Your cookbook is private

Everything you save stays on your iPhone. There are no public profiles, no feeds and no server of ours where saved recipes are collected. We can’t see your cookbook.

What the app does when you save a recipe

  • Websites: your iPhone reads the recipe page, like a browser, and keeps the ingredients, the steps, the main photo and a link to the original page. You can remove or replace the photo before saving.
  • Books and screenshots: the text is recognised on your iPhone so you don’t have to retype it.
  • Instagram, TikTok, Facebook, YouTube, Pinterest: “Share → Twelve Geese” keeps the link to the post. The app doesn’t download videos and doesn’t read posts automatically — you paste the description or add a screenshot yourself. The link stays in the recipe so you can always go back to the original.

The original author stays the author

Recipes, photos, texts and videos belong to the people who created them. Saving a recipe for your own cooking doesn’t make it yours to publish. That’s why Twelve Geese always keeps the source link, and when you share a recipe, the link goes with it while photos taken automatically from websites stay out.

Good habits

  • Save recipes for your own cooking and share them with people close to you.
  • Keep the credit: when you share, leave the link to the original author.
  • Don’t republish other people’s recipes, photos or texts as your own.
  • Support the creators you love: watch, like and follow the original posts.

Questions

If you are a recipe author or rights holder and have a question about Twelve Geese, write to twelvegeese@gmail.com. See also our Terms of Use and Privacy Policy.
