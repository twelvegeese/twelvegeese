Privacy Policy

Twelve Geese is a seasonal recipe app and a personal cookbook for iPhone. This policy explains, in plain words, what happens with your information. In short: the app has no accounts and no server of ours for your recipes, and we cannot see your cookbook.

Who we are

Twelve Geese is developed by [developer’s full name / business name], [address], Poland (“we”, “us”). Contact: twelvegeese@gmail.com.

What we collect

We do not collect personal data through the app. There is no sign-up, no advertising, no analytics and no tracking, and the app contains no third-party analytics or advertising code. The only personal data we receive is what you send us yourself, for example when you email support.

Your cookbook stays on your iPhone

Your own recipes, their photos, your notes, favourites, the “Cooking” plan, the calendar of cooked dishes and your shopping list are stored only on your device, inside the app. We have no access to them and they are never sent to us.

If iCloud Backup or a backup to your computer is turned on, iOS includes this data in your own device backup. Those backups are made and kept by Apple under Apple’s privacy policy and your Apple account settings.

If you delete the app, its data is deleted from your device. You can make your own backup file at any time in the app (Settings → Back Up My Cookbook); you decide where that file is saved and who receives it.

Importing recipes

From a web link. When you import a recipe from a link, your iPhone connects directly to that website — the same way a browser does — and reads the page and its main picture. The website receives a normal request from your device (including your IP address) and handles it under its own policies. Nothing passes through us.

From social networks. When you use “Share → Twelve Geese” in another app (for example Instagram, TikTok, Facebook, YouTube or Pinterest), Twelve Geese receives only the link and any text that app shares with it. The link is kept in a folder on your device that only Twelve Geese can use, until you open the app. Twelve Geese does not open or read social network posts automatically; you add the recipe text yourself.

From photos and screenshots. Text in photos and screenshots is recognised on your device using Apple’s built-in technology. The pictures are not sent to us or to anyone else.

Pasting text. The app reads the clipboard only when you tap “Paste” or “Paste text”.

Permissions

  • Camera — only when you choose to take a photo of a dish or scan a page.
  • Photos — only the pictures you pick yourself, through the standard iOS photo picker.
  • Reminders — only if you choose to send your shopping list to Apple Reminders; the app adds products to the list you select.

You can change these permissions at any time in the iPhone Settings app.

Subscription

Subscriptions are sold and managed by Apple through the App Store. We never receive your payment details. The app checks your subscription status, and reads Apple’s signed record of your app download to check dates, directly on your device using Apple’s StoreKit. Apple provides developers with sales reports that do not identify you personally.

Sharing recipes

When you share a recipe, it is sent through the standard iOS Share menu to the app and people you choose. We do not store or see what you share.

Support emails

If you write to us, we receive your email address and your message. We use them only to answer you and keep them only as long as needed for that.

Our website

twelvegeese.com is hosted on GitHub Pages and uses Google Fonts. When you visit it, GitHub and Google may receive technical information such as your IP address, under their own privacy policies. [To review: consider hosting the font on the site itself.]

Children

The app does not collect personal data from anyone, including children.

Your rights

Under data protection law (including the GDPR) you have rights such as access to and deletion of your personal data. Because the app’s data is stored only on your device, you control it directly; for support emails, write to us and we will help. You may also lodge a complaint with a data protection authority — in Poland, the President of the Personal Data Protection Office (UODO).

Changes

If the app or this policy changes, we will update this page and the date at the top.
